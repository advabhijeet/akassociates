MSME Recovery

Plain-English, document-first guidance for MSME delayed-payment recovery, unpaid invoices, Udyam records, MSEFC proceedings, arbitration, award challenge, enforcement and buyer-side response. Chambers of AK is based in Patna and develops its MSME practice for matters connected with Bihar and Delhi, subject to the statutory forum, transaction record and professional feasibility in each case.

MSME Recovery Route Map

1. Document Review

Udyam status, purchase order, invoices, delivery proof, ledger, part-payments, buyer objections and correspondence are arranged before choosing the recovery route.

2. Demand & Reconciliation

The first stage may involve payment reminders, account reconciliation, demand communication, settlement discussion or preparation for formal statutory action.

3. MSEFC / Arbitration Route

Where the MSME delayed-payment framework applies, the matter may move through the Facilitation Council process, including conciliation and arbitration-linked steps.

4. Award / Challenge / Execution

After adjudication, the next question may involve award compliance, challenge, deposit requirement, enforcement, execution, settlement or defence strategy.

When This Matter Usually Arises

MSME delayed payment matters usually begin with unpaid invoices, delayed buyer payments, ledger mismatch, supply disputes, quality objections, or refusal to clear dues after goods or services are delivered.

Chambers of AK is based in Patna and assesses MSME payment recovery and response matters connected with Bihar and Delhi. The correct MSEFC or later court stage is determined by the statute and the facts; the page does not treat Delhi NCR as one single forum or assume that the buyer's location alone decides jurisdiction.

Eligibility And Enterprise Records

Enterprise Status And Registration Timing

Keep the enterprise classification, registration date, contract date and supply or service dates together. Registration timing is presently a live legal issue: earlier Supreme Court decisions treated later registration as prospective, while NBCC (India) Ltd. v. State of West Bengal (2025) took a different view on access to Section 18 and referred the conflict to a larger Bench. Current authority should therefore be checked before treating registration timing as an automatic bar or entitlement.

Buyer And Transaction

Identify the buyer, supply or service, invoice-wise dues, work order or purchase order, delivery records, part payments and any dispute raised before or after delivery.

Claim Readiness

A claim should not depend only on invoice totals. It should be supported by transaction chronology, account statements, communications and a clear calculation of principal and interest.

Limitation And Date Sensitivity

Do Not Ignore Limitation

The Supreme Court in 2025 confirmed that the Limitation Act applies when a Section 18 dispute proceeds to arbitration. Old invoices should therefore be checked invoice-wise for due dates, acknowledgements, part-payments and other limitation facts before assuming that an arbitral recovery remains available.

Acceptance And Due Dates

The date of supply, acceptance or deemed acceptance, any written payment date and the statutory payment ceiling should be recorded invoice-wise. These dates affect Section 15 payment analysis and Section 16 interest calculations.

Acknowledgements And Payments

Part-payments and written acknowledgements may affect limitation depending on their wording and timing. Debit notes, credit notes and account reconciliations should also be preserved rather than treated as mere accounting entries.

Forum And MSEFC Route

For a statutory reference under Section 18 of the MSMED Act, the supplier's location is central to MSEFC jurisdiction: the Council or referred centre may deal with a dispute where the supplier is located within its jurisdiction even if the buyer is located elsewhere in India.

That statutory forum rule should be checked separately from the merits of the transaction. Purchase orders, delivery or service records, contractual terms, invoice-wise dues, limitation, buyer objections and any existing proceedings still matter to the claim or defence.

For Bihar and Delhi enquiries, first identify the supplier's registered business location for the relevant transaction and the Council already approached, if any. Delhi, Noida, Ghaziabad and Gurugram should not be treated as one MSEFC jurisdiction merely because they form part of the wider NCR market.

Legal Framework

Section 15: Payment Timeline

Where a payment date is agreed in writing, the agreed period cannot exceed forty-five days from acceptance or deemed acceptance. If there is no written payment date, the appointed-day rule under Section 15 must be checked instead of simply applying forty-five days to every invoice.

Section 16: Statutory Interest

If payment is not made as required by Section 15, Section 16 provides compound interest with monthly rests at three times the RBI bank rate. The start date and any part-payments should be calculated invoice-wise.

Section 18: MSEFC Process

The currently operative framework provides for conciliation and, if settlement is not reached, arbitration. The supplier-location rule is important when identifying the competent Council.

Section 19: Challenge Deposit

A non-supplier applicant seeking to set aside a Council or referred-institution award ordinarily faces the statutory requirement to deposit seventy-five per cent of the amount in terms of the award or order, subject to the court's directions under Section 19.

Documents To Keep Ready

MSME Records

  • Udyam registration certificate/details.
  • Enterprise classification record.
  • GST details, if relevant.
  • Business correspondence and registration records.

Transaction Papers

  • Purchase orders, work orders or contracts.
  • Invoices, delivery challans and completion records.
  • Ledger statement and account confirmation.
  • Bank payment records and part-payment entries.

Dispute And Recovery Record

  • Payment reminders and demand communications.
  • Email or WhatsApp acknowledgement.
  • Quality objections or buyer replies.
  • Any council filing, notice, order or award.
Supplier And Buyer Sides

For Suppliers

The focus is on proving enterprise status, supply or service, invoice amount, acceptance, delivery, payment reminders, account balance and the unpaid principal amount.

For Buyers

Response strategy may involve checking supply disputes, quality issues, invoice mismatch, payment already made, limitation, council jurisdiction, contractual terms and supporting records.

For Pending Matters

If a filing is already pending, the application, notice, reply, conciliation record, hearing status, award, challenge or enforcement stage should be identified before the next step.

Common Mistakes

Unclear Claim Amount

Invoice totals, part payments, debit notes, GST, interest and ledger balance should be separated clearly before filing or replying.

Weak Delivery Proof

Claims become harder when supply or service completion is not supported by challans, emails, acceptance records, work completion proof or acknowledgements.

Ignoring Forum Stage

Conciliation, arbitration, award, challenge and enforcement are different stages. Strategy should match the current procedural stage.

Official References

For primary reference, see the MSMED Act, 2006 on India Code, the official MSME Samadhaan information page, the Supreme Court's 2025 NBCC (India) Ltd. judgment on the registration issue and larger-Bench reference, and the 2025 judgment in Sonali Power Equipments Pvt. Ltd. on limitation in Section 18 proceedings.

The MSMED (Amendment) Act, 2026 has received Presidential assent. Section 1(2) provides that its provisions come into force on date or dates appointed by the Central Government through Gazette notification. The commencement status of any amended provision should therefore be verified before it is applied to a live matter.

FAQ

Can every MSME file before MSEFC?

The delayed payment route is generally connected with micro and small enterprises and valid registration records. Eligibility depends on documents and transaction facts.

Why is forty-five days important?

Under the MSMED framework, a written payment period cannot exceed forty-five days from acceptance or deemed acceptance. The actual date chart should still be checked carefully.

Can a buyer defend an MSME claim?

Yes. A buyer may raise legally available defences based on supply, quality, payment record, invoice mismatch, jurisdiction, limitation, or other transaction documents.
