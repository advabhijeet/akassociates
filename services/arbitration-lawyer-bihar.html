Arbitration Lawyer in Bihar: Patna Court Procedure and Dispute Stages

How the arbitration clause, seat and court competence affect proceedings connected with Bihar, including notices, appointments, urgent protection, challenges and enforcement.

Procedural Overview

Before the Tribunal

Review the written arbitration agreement, seat, appointment method, Section 21 invocation, limitation and whether urgent court measures are necessary.

During the Arbitration

Evaluate statement of claim, defence, counterclaim, jurisdiction objections, tribunal evidence, and Section 17 interim relief.

After the Award

Check signed award receipt, Section 34 grounds and limitation, Section 36 enforcement and any separate stay order.

Bihar and Patna Court Jurisdiction

Does a contract connected to Patna necessarily belong in Patna High Court?

No. The location of the parties, a contract signed in Bihar, or an arbitration hearing held in Patna does not alone resolve the proper court. The first questions are whether the arbitration is domestic or international, the juridical seat, any exclusive jurisdiction clause, the subject matter, and the precise relief requested. An agreement describing Patna as a hearing venue may require interpretation before it can be treated as the legal seat.

Section 2(1)(e) of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 defines the relevant 'Court'. For a qualifying commercial dispute of specified value, Section 10 of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015 may allocate arbitration proceedings to a designated Commercial Court or Commercial Division. Thus, a Section 9 interim petition or a Section 34 challenge should not automatically be addressed to Patna High Court merely because the transaction took place in Bihar.

Domestic Section 11 appointment applications may come before the Patna High Court where the relevant seat, law and other requirements establish its competence; applications under Sections 9, 34 or 36 may follow different jurisdictional routes. A Patna seat does not eliminate the need to identify the specific statutory forum.

Clause, Seat and Section 21 Notice

1. Read the complete clause before sending an invocation notice

Obtain the contract, annexures, later amendments and applicable purchase orders. Read any seat, venue, governing law, institution, number of arbitrators, nomination mechanism, exclusive jurisdiction and escalation requirements together. A clause may name one city for hearings while conferring supervisory jurisdiction elsewhere; a party should not select a convenient court without resolving that distinction. For a detailed method, read the arbitration clause checklist.

2. Section 21 turns on a request received by the respondent

Unless otherwise agreed, arbitral proceedings commence when the respondent receives a request that the particular dispute be referred to arbitration. An ordinary invoice reminder may not amount to a Section 21 invocation. Preserve the exact notice, all invoked agreements and disputes, delivery records, replies and the applicable limitation chronology. Pre-arbitration negotiation or mediation requirements must also be examined.

Invocation can be significant for limitation but does not automatically revive a stale claim. Note the original contractual breach, last payment, acknowledgement, relevant demands and subsequent correspondence. Our Section 21 notice guide explains receipt, appointment and multiple-contract problems.

Section 11 and Patna High Court

3. Arbitrator appointment after a mechanism fails

Section 11 becomes relevant when the agreed appointment procedure is not followed or a statutory ground for court intervention arises. Record each appointment proposal, communication and the timing of any failure. Test the proposed arbitrator's eligibility, independence and disclosure obligations under Section 12. Do not assume that a party can appoint a sole arbitrator unilaterally merely because a form contract says so; the actual clause and current authorities matter.

The Patna High Court's rules directory publishes the Patna High Court Arbitration Centre Rules, 2023, notified through the Bihar Gazette in May 2024, and a later amendment. The Arbitration Centre's administration is not automatically a mandatory court forum for every Section 11 appointment or every private arbitration. Consult the applicable current rules and court directions rather than an outdated fee or filing template.

Interim Measures

4. Section 9 court protection and Section 17 tribunal relief

Section 9 may permit urgent court measures before, during or in certain circumstances after an arbitration, including protective directions about property, assets or evidence. The petition must establish the proper forum, concrete urgency, the identified risk and the measure sought. A disputed money claim alone does not automatically justify a freeze or security deposit. If Section 9 relief is ordered before arbitration begins, the Act ordinarily requires arbitral proceedings to commence within 90 days of the order, or within further time fixed by the court.

Once a tribunal has been constituted, Section 9(3) generally directs the parties to an adequate Section 17 tribunal remedy unless circumstances render that remedy inefficacious. Check whether the tribunal is functioning and can provide effective protection. For proof and relief drafting, read the Section 9 interim-relief guide.

Tribunal Pleadings and Award

5. Claims, defence and counterclaims

Prepare a contract-wise chronology, statement of claim, reconciled account, interest basis, factual grounds and indexed evidence. Respondents should examine jurisdiction objections, limitation, breach, mitigation, credits, set-off and counterclaim, then track the tribunal's pleading and evidence timetable. Preserve the arbitration agreement, interim orders, statements, witness materials and procedural objections; post-award review cannot be reconstructed reliably from the final award alone.

6. Section 34 challenges are time-sensitive and limited in scope

A Section 34 application is not a general rehearing on the merits. It requires statutory setting-aside grounds and a competent court. Under Section 34(3), the basic period is three months from receipt of the signed award (or disposal of a qualifying Section 33 request), with only a further 30 days on sufficient cause, but not thereafter. Avoid describing this as an unconditional 120-day filing period. Determine the actual date of receipt, any court closure issue and applicable procedural rule immediately.

For the detailed law, see the three-month and thirty-day limitation guide and Section 34 documentary checklist.

7. A Section 34 filing does not automatically stay execution

Under Section 36, an award may be enforced subject to the statutory position and any separate stay. Merely filing a challenge does not by itself suspend enforcement. A party seeking compliance or resisting execution should obtain the operative award, proof of receipt, credit/payment record, asset information, Section 34 filing details and any stay order before advising on enforcement.

MSME Arbitration Distinction

8. An MSEFC statutory reference is not an ordinary Section 11 matter

Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council proceedings under Section 18 of the MSMED Act have a special conciliation and arbitration framework. The presence of an ordinary arbitration clause does not by itself resolve that statutory route. Sections 18–19, supplier eligibility, Council jurisdiction, statutory interest, limitation and pre-deposit questions require separate assessment. Frequently Asked Questions

Does every Bihar arbitration case go directly to Patna High Court?

No. Section 11 domestic appointments may fall before Patna High Court when jurisdiction exists. Section 9 and Section 34 applications may be dealt with by the competent district or Commercial Court.

Does the hearing venue decide the legal seat?

Not automatically. The seat must be assessed from the entire clause, surrounding contractual terms and applicable judicial principles.

Does a Section 34 challenge stop enforcement?

No automatic stay follows merely from filing. Examine Section 36 and the actual operative stay order, if any.
