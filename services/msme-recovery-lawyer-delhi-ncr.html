MSME Recovery

MSME Recovery Lawyer in Delhi NCR

For MSME delayed payment, unpaid invoices, buyer objections, MSEFC proceedings and enforcement-related enquiries connected with Delhi and the wider NCR market, this page explains the documents, dates and forum questions that usually matter.

When This Page Helps

Unpaid Invoices

You are a micro or small supplier with Delhi/NCR-linked transactions and need to organise invoice-wise dues, delivery proof, payment terms and the supplier's location relevant to Section 18 before selecting the statutory forum.

MSEFC Preparation

You want to assess Udyam registration, buyer details, purchase orders, delivery proof, limitation and the correct delayed-payment claim record.

Buyer Response

You have received an MSME claim or notice and need to review quality objections, payments, debit notes, reconciliation records, jurisdiction and contract papers.

Delhi And NCR Focus

Delhi NCR is one commercial region, but it is not one MSEFC jurisdiction. Delhi, Noida and Ghaziabad in Uttar Pradesh, and Gurugram and Faridabad in Haryana, fall under different State or Union Territory systems.

Under the currently operative Section 18 framework, the supplier's location is central to MSEFC jurisdiction and the buyer may be located anywhere in India. A Delhi supplier may therefore have a Delhi MSEFC route even where the buyer is outside Delhi; a supplier located in Noida or Gurugram should not assume that the Delhi Council is the correct forum merely because the transaction is part of NCR commerce.

Chambers of AK is based in Patna and reviews Delhi/NCR MSME recovery enquiries in a document-first manner, subject to the correct statutory forum, transaction record and professional feasibility.

Documents To Keep Ready

MSME And Party Records

  • Udyam Registration Certificate and enterprise profile.
  • Supplier and buyer legal names, GST details and addresses.
  • Purchase order, work order, contract or email confirmation.
  • Jurisdiction or arbitration clause, if any.

Transaction Papers

  • Invoice-wise outstanding table.
  • Delivery challans, e-way bills or completion records.
  • Ledger, bank entries, part payments and debit notes.
  • Email, WhatsApp or portal communications.

Dispute Record

  • Payment reminders and demand communications.
  • Buyer objections, rejection notices or quality complaints.
  • Legal notice, reply notice or settlement discussions.
  • MSEFC application, reply, order or award if already pending.
Issues To Assess

MSME recovery preparation should check supplier status and registration date, the relevant supply or service, the written payment period, acceptance or deemed acceptance, buyer objections, invoice-wise dues, limitation and the correct MSEFC.

Registration timing is a live legal issue. Earlier Supreme Court decisions treated later registration as prospective, while NBCC (India) Ltd. v. State of West Bengal (2025) took a different view on access to Section 18 and referred the conflict to a larger Bench. The registration record, contract and supply dates, and current binding authority should therefore be checked together rather than treating registration date alone as decisive.

The Supreme Court confirmed in 2025 that the Limitation Act applies when a Section 18 dispute proceeds to arbitration. Older invoices should therefore be examined for due dates, acknowledgements and part-payments instead of assuming that every old MSME debt remains capable of an arbitral award.

First Enquiry Format

Send a short note with supplier name, Udyam registration date, buyer name and location, total outstanding amount, invoice-wise summary, last payment date, buyer objections, supply or service location, contract clause position and whether any MSEFC or court proceeding has started.
