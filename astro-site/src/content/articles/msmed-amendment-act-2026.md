## The amendment is not a single-date change

The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act No. 16 of 2026) received Presidential assent and was published on 13 August 2026. Section 1(2) permits different commencement dates for different provisions. The Ministry of MSME subsequently issued S.O. 5575(E) on 12 October 2026.

The important distinction is between enactment, commencement and practical implementation. A provision may be enacted but not commenced; a commenced provision may still require rules, notifications or administrative arrangements for its operation.

This article is reviewed as of 28 November 2026 against the Gazette text and the commencement notification. No subsequent notification commencing Amendment Act sections 8–10 has been verified in the sources reviewed. This is not a certification that no further notification exists. Check the latest Gazette before taking procedural action.

On the review date, 15 December 2026 is still in the future. The statutory composition change is therefore scheduled, not yet effective under this notification.

## Registration and classification: Sections 7 and 8

The substituted Section 7(1) authorises classification by notification using both investment in plant and machinery or equipment and turnover. The amended text excludes certain costs of pollution control, research and development, industrial safety devices and other notified items from the relevant investment calculation. Actual monetary classification thresholds must be checked against the applicable notification; the Amendment Act itself does not prescribe a new table of rupee limits.

The substituted Section 8 requires the Central Government to notify a national digital platform for free and voluntary registration. States may notify corresponding State platforms and may also extend applicable scheme benefits to enterprises registered through the national mechanism. Amendment Act section 2 adjusts the classification-related definitions and adds the Development Commissioner definition; it does not expressly rewrite the supplier definition in Section 2(n).

Registration being voluntary in general does not necessarily establish retrospective entitlement to special delayed-payment remedies. Contract dates, actual supplies, classification history and the pending controversy addressed in NBCC remain important. See our [registration timing analysis](/updates/msme-registration-after-contract-supply.html) and [Udyam registration guide](/updates/msme-registration-benefits-eligibility.html).

## Mandatory invoice settlement through TReDS: Section 15A

The new Section 15A is one of the provisions commenced on 15 October 2026. It requires Central Public Sector Enterprises procuring goods or services from micro, small and medium enterprises to route invoice settlement through a Trade Receivables Discounting System platform authorised by the Reserve Bank, in the form and manner prescribed by the Central Government.

There is also an earlier, separate administrative mandate. S.O. 3507(E), dated 30 June 2026, issued under Sections 9 and 10 of the principal Act, requires operating CPSEs to route MSME invoice settlement through an RBI-authorised TReDS platform. The Ministry's [10 July 2026 PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2283195&lang=1&reg=6) confirms this requirement. The notification text reproduced in the Gazette index expressly distinguishes mandatory routing from compulsory discounting: the MSME supplier retains the choice whether to obtain receivables financing. It also requires prescribed disclosure and an auditor's compliance certificate for operating CPSEs. Thus 15 October is not the first date on which the CPSE routing obligation arose. Check the original June notification, its continued operation under the saving provision and subsequent instructions applicable to the buyer.

The Central Government may notify other authorities, bodies or entities to come within the mechanism. State Governments have corresponding notification powers for State Public Sector Enterprises and other bodies or entities, with State-prescribed procedures.

The distinction matters: the Act does not automatically place every private buyer under the same obligation merely because the buyer purchases from an MSME. Identify the buyer's legal status, any applicable notification and the operative rules governing routing and settlement. Check actual platform onboarding and payment arrangements.

The new Section 22A complements Section 15A with disclosure obligations for the Central and State categories specified in the respective provisions. The reporting framework expressly refers to MSME invoices routed and settled through TReDS.

The [RBI Trade Receivables Discounting System Directions, 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13526&Mode=0), dated 23 June 2026, provide the separate regulatory framework for platform operators and participants. TReDS facilitates trade receivable financing and discounting; it does not by itself determine the existence of a disputed debt or guarantee that every invoice will be financed. Nor does the amendment expressly replace the existing payment period under Section 15 or the interest formula under Section 16. See the [15-day and 45-day payment rules](/updates/msme-45-days-payment-rule.html) and [Section 16 compound interest guide](/updates/section-16-msmed-act-compound-interest.html).

## Changes to dispute resolution: read Section 18 with the Mediation Act

Amendment Act section 8 amends Section 18 as substituted by Section 62 read with the Seventh Schedule of the Mediation Act, 2023. The proposed operational sequence includes the following:

- Completion of mediation within 90 days from the date fixed for first appearance.
- The Council's action to take up or refer arbitration within 30 days of termination of unsuccessful mediation.
- Making the award within 90 days from completion of pleadings.
- Territorial jurisdiction linked to the supplier's official address under Section 8 registration, with the buyer located anywhere in India.
- An enabling provision for a notified online dispute-resolution mechanism and prescribed procedures, including electronic pleadings, evidence and audio-video proceedings.

These are enacted changes, not deadlines demonstrated to be operative on the review date. S.O. 5575(E) does not commence Amendment Act section 8. In addition, the 9 October 2023 commencement notification S.O. 4384(E) under the Mediation Act did not include Section 62. The operation of the underlying substitution requires independent examination of later notifications. A further search on 28 November 2026 did not establish commencement of Section 62; the absence of a verified later instrument is not proof that none exists.

The existing Council process should not be described as universally replaced merely because the amended language appears in a Gazette or State rules. Identify the law applicable to the date and stage of the particular reference. The actual MSME ODR filing portal is a separate administrative development: its use is not proof that every proposed amendment to Section 18 is in force. See our [MSME ODR portal guide](/updates/msme-odr-portal-filing-procedure.html) and [Council proceedings guide](/updates/msme-facilitation-council-process.html).

## New recovery mechanism: Section 18A

Amendment Act section 9 inserts Section 18A. Once legally operative, its text permits recovery of mediated settlements and arbitral awards as arrears of land revenue through the District Collector, Deputy Commissioner or other State-notified authority where the buyer's assets are located.

It also recognises the determined amount as a valid and legally enforceable debt capable of recognition under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. This does not automatically establish a right to commence insolvency proceedings in every case: the applicable IBC requirements, thresholds, standing, limitation and other conditions must independently be satisfied.

No commencement date for Amendment Act section 9 appears in S.O. 5575(E). A supplier should not abandon the existing enforcement route in anticipation of the land-revenue mechanism. Verify commencement, transitional applicability and the competent recovery authority before filing.

## Award challenges and the 75% pre-deposit: Section 19

The existing MSMED Act already contains a 75% deposit requirement for applications by a party other than a supplier, subject to its statutory scope and judicial interpretation. The amendment does not create that requirement for the first time.

The substituted Section 19, enacted through Amendment Act section 10, extends its wording to mediated settlement agreements, specifies the court by reference to the supplier's official address, and provides for release of part of the deposited amount to the supplier during the challenge. If the application remains pending for more than six months, the new proviso requires payment to the supplier of at least 50% of the awarded amount from the deposit.

For clarity, the denominator in the proviso is the awarded amount, not merely the 75% deposit. On an illustrative award of Rs 10 lakh, a 75% deposit would be Rs 7.5 lakh, while 50% of the award would be Rs 5 lakh. This example describes the enacted wording only, not an immediately enforceable entitlement.

S.O. 5575(E) does not appoint a commencement date for Amendment Act section 10. Do not use the substituted Section 19 to determine the forum, deposit release, limitation or stay in a current award challenge without establishing its applicability. See [post-award challenges and enforcement](/updates/after-msefc-award-challenge-deposit-stay-enforcement.html).

## Additional Councils and revised composition

The substituted Section 20, effective under the notification on 15 October 2026, requires State Governments to establish an adequate number of Councils in addition to existing Councils, through notifications specifying locations, jurisdiction and areas. It also requires regular meetings and enables provision of appropriate infrastructure and personnel.

The substituted Section 21 is scheduled to commence on 15 December 2026. It retains a three-to-five-member composition and specifies a Chairperson not below Joint Director rank, representation from micro or small industry or enterprise associations, and at least one member from the legal field. The State prescribes composition, vacancies and procedures within the statutory framework. A statutory direction to establish Councils is not proof that each new Council has actually been constituted and is accepting references.

## Penalties and adjudication

The substituted Sections 27 and 27A commenced on 15 October 2026 under Amendment Act section 14. The following distinctions are important:

| Contravention | First instance | Later instances |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wilfully false registration information or non-compliance with Section 26(2) | Warning | Rs 1,000–50,000 for second or subsequent instances |
| Buyer contravention of Section 22 | Warning | Rs 10,000–50,000 for second; fine of Rs 50,000–1 lakh for third or later contraventions |

Section 27(3) provides for notified increases by 10% of the minimum penalty amount after every three years from commencement. Section 27A provides for appointment of the Development Commissioner as adjudicating officer, reasonable opportunity to be heard, an appeal to the Secretary within 30 days of receipt of the order (with possible condonation for sufficient cause), disposal of appeal within 60 days of filing, and land-revenue recovery of unpaid penalties.

The penalty recovery provision must not be confused with the separately enacted Section 18A for settlement agreements and arbitral awards. The former is commenced by S.O. 5575(E); the latter is not. Actual adjudicating appointment, prescribed procedure and any prosecution mechanism for the statutory fine require separate verification. Nor should Section 27 be described as imposing these penalties for every delayed invoice or every TReDS issue.

Amendment Act sections 15 and 16 expand Central and State rule-making powers under Sections 29 and 30 respectively. Section 17 preserves earlier actions and notifications insofar as consistent with the amendment until revoked. It is not a blanket rule applying every new substantive entitlement to historical transactions.

## Bihar's Rules, 2026: the commencement issue in practice

The Bihar Gazette dated 29 October 2026 (Extraordinary No. 1320) publishes revised MSEFC Rules that supersede the 2007 Rules and provide for District Councils and a State Apex Council. Rule 9 assigns intra-Bihar buyer disputes to the supplier's district and disputes involving buyers outside Bihar to the State Council. Rule 14 introduces a processing-fee reference to the Fourth Schedule of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.

The published Rules expressly state immediate effect from Gazette publication under Rule 1(ii). The State Council and District Council allocation is set out in Rule 9, and physical filing remains an option under Rule 10. Confirm actual constitution, filing arrangements and any case-specific directions before relying on the new allocation. The published Rules also reproduce several features of the enacted Central changes to mediation, arbitration, enforcement, challenge jurisdiction and Council composition. Their publication does not itself commence the corresponding Central provisions. Assess delegated legislative authority, compatibility with operative Central law, actual constitution of Councils and the applicable transition on a provision-by-provision basis. Do not assume either automatic validity of every procedure or invalidity of the whole instrument without legal analysis.

Read our [Bihar MSEFC procedure update](/updates/bihar-msefc-filing-procedure-msme-delayed-payment.html).

## Practical compliance and litigation checklist

For suppliers, preserve the relevant registration, contract and supply dates; identify whether the buyer falls under notified TReDS requirements; track invoice acceptance, principal, statutory interest and payment evidence. For buyers, audit the procurement category, prescribed routing and reporting arrangements, financial-statement disclosures and records of disputed invoices.

For ongoing Council cases or award challenges, identify the exact commencement provision, current procedural stage, actual Council constitution and applicable saving provisions. Check every claimed deadline against the operative statutory text rather than assuming the date of Presidential assent is the date all procedural changes took effect.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the whole MSMED Amendment Act, 2026 in force?

No single commencement date applies to the entire Act under S.O. 5575(E). It commences the specified provisions from 15 October, schedules section 12 for 15 December and does not appoint dates for sections 8–10.

### Has the 45-day payment limit changed?

The Amendment Act does not amend the payment period in Section 15 or the interest formula in Section 16. Its new Section 15A creates a distinct TReDS routing framework.

### Can I recover an MSEFC award through the Collector immediately?

The new Section 18A is enacted but not commenced by S.O. 5575(E). Check subsequent commencement notifications and the applicable recovery procedure before invoking it.

### Is the 50% release after six months already mandatory?

The new proviso is part of substituted Section 19; Amendment Act section 10 was not commenced by the October notification. The existing Section 19 and applicable judicial decisions must be examined for present relief.
