Quick Answer: What Papers Are Needed?

For a proposed commercial recovery suit in India, organise the underlying contract and transaction records, proof of supply or services, an invoice-wise payment reconciliation, correspondence, a limitation chronology, and any pre-institution mediation records. Filing also requires a properly framed plaint, document disclosure under the commercial-suit version of Order XI CPC, a Statement of Truth, and applicable authority and court-fee papers.

Important distinction: a client's evidence checklist is not the same as the documents required by the court registry. Not every unpaid business invoice automatically becomes a commercial suit. First examine the nature of the dispute, specified value, territorial jurisdiction, any arbitration clause and the relevant court rules.

Use the sections below as a preparation checklist, not as a substitute for the statutory requirements or local filing directions applicable to the chosen court.

1. Confirm That the Commercial Court Is the Correct Forum

Nature of dispute: Section 2(1)(c) of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015 defines commercial disputes, including certain ordinary transactions of merchants and traders and other listed commercial relationships. The amount due is not, by itself, enough to establish that the dispute is commercial.

Specified value: the statutory floor is Rs. 3 lakh, subject to any applicable higher notified value and the court's jurisdiction. For a claim seeking recovery of money, Section 12 includes interest calculated up to the institution date when determining specified value. Do not confuse specified value with the final amount decreed or with a bare principal-only figure.

Forum and relief: record the parties' places of business, where the contract was performed, contractual jurisdiction clauses, arbitration agreement, and any urgency requiring an interim order. A matter may instead require ordinary civil proceedings, arbitration or a distinct statutory remedy such as an MSME delayed-payment reference, depending on the facts.

2. Assemble the Transaction and Outstanding-Dues Record

Contract and party records

  • Executed agreement, accepted quotation, purchase order or work order, with annexures and amendments.
  • Names, legal status, registration particulars and addresses of the contracting parties.
  • Payment, interest, dispute resolution, arbitration, jurisdiction and notice clauses.
  • Proof of the signatory's authority where relevant.

Performance and delivery

  • Invoices, delivery challans, transport receipts, e-way bills, goods-receipt notes or acknowledgements.
  • Work-completion certificates, inspection or acceptance records, milestone records and service reports, as applicable.
  • Relevant GST records, credit notes, debit notes, returns and reconciliation material.
  • Quality objections, returns, rejection notices, deductions and replacement communications, even if adverse.

Payment and admissions

  • Invoice-wise ledger, bank entries and payment receipts mapped to the correct invoice.
  • Balance confirmations, signed acknowledgements and correspondence discussing payment.
  • Details of advances, retentions, debit adjustments, part-payments, settled items and disputed balances.
  • Demand notices, dispatch and delivery proof, replies, settlement communications and earlier proceedings.

Practical point: retain the complete chain of documents, not just documents favourable to recovery. A ledger may summarise the claim, but it should be supported by evidence of the transaction, delivery or work, payment terms and appropriation of payments.

3. Check Section 12A Pre-Institution Mediation

Section 12A of the Commercial Courts Act requires exhaustion of pre-institution mediation before instituting a commercial suit that does not contemplate urgent interim relief. Depending on the applicable procedure and outcome, retain the mediation application, fee record, notices, proof of service and the resulting non-starter or failure report, or signed settlement document.

In Patil Automation Pvt. Ltd. v. Rakheja Engineers Pvt. Ltd. (17 August 2022), the Supreme Court held the Section 12A requirement mandatory and stated that a non-compliant plaint is liable to rejection under Order VII Rule 11, with the declaration operating prospectively from 20 August 2022.

The urgent-interim-relief exception is not an invitation to insert a routine injunction prayer. In Yamini Manohar v. T.K.D. Keerthi (13 October 2023), the Supreme Court explained that the court can examine the plaint, cause of action and relief to determine whether genuine urgency is contemplated. Preserve documents evidencing any actual threatened prejudice.

Do not assume that an ordinary demand notice substitutes for Section 12A mediation. Equally, a separate Section 12A process does not apply mechanically to every debt claim or every dispute-resolution route; classify the proposed proceeding first.

4. Prepare the Plaint and Court-Filing Papers

  • Plaint and memo of parties: identify the cause of action, jurisdictional facts, limitation basis, transaction chronology, precisely calculated relief and any interim prayer.
  • Statement of Truth: verification by affidavit under Order VI Rule 15A CPC in the form prescribed for commercial disputes, by a qualified deponent with knowledge and due authority.
  • Order XI disclosure: document list, copies, necessary declarations and particulars detailed below. Do not treat the list as a mere set of numbered annexures.
  • Authorisation: where applicable, board resolution, partnership/firm documents, power of attorney or other authority for institution and verification; vakalatnama for counsel.
  • Valuation and fees: statement of suit valuation, interest computation, appropriate court-fee material and process-fee/filing requirements under applicable rules.
  • Interim applications: supporting affidavit and contemporaneous evidence where urgent orders are genuinely sought.
  • Registry requirements: index, pagination, readable copies, correct exhibits/document references, translations, e-filing format and any locally prescribed synopsis or list of dates.

The exact filing package can vary by court, party type and relief. Check the competent Commercial Court's current practice directions rather than assuming a uniform annexure format throughout India.

5. Order XI Disclosure: More Than a Favourable Document Bundle

The commercial-suit version of Order XI Rule 1 CPC requires the plaintiff to file a list and copies of documents within its power, possession, control or custody pertaining to the suit, including documents that may be adverse to its case. This is broader than attaching the invoices relied upon in the plaint.

Under Rule 1(2), the list should indicate whether each document is an original, office copy or photocopy and briefly identify the parties, mode of execution, issuance or receipt and line of custody. The Statement of Truth also addresses complete disclosure. Rules governing urgent filings and later production of omitted documents must be considered; there is no unrestricted right to introduce withheld documents whenever convenient.

If a relevant original is with the defendant or another person, identify it and preserve the basis for seeking its production. Missing records, adverse correspondence and inconsistencies should be addressed with counsel before filing rather than concealed.

6. Preserve Electronic Evidence Properly

Emails, WhatsApp messages, ERP exports, electronic ledgers and digital invoices should be preserved with dates, sender or account identifiers, complete conversation context and the source device or system where possible. A cropped screenshot may omit material context and invite an authenticity dispute.

Where applicable, Sections 61 to 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 govern electronic-record evidence, including certification requirements under Section 63. Confirm which evidence statute and transitional regime apply to the proceeding. Keep original records available for authentication and prepare the prescribed certificate where required; an informal screenshot is not automatically a substitute.

7. Create an Invoice-Wise Reconciliation and Interest Worksheet

Use one worksheet for the entire claim and preserve the underlying records for each entry. The following is a blank illustrative structure, not a statement of amounts due in any actual case.

Total-[Sum][Sum][Balance]Reconciled statement

Separate principal, contractual or statutory interest, tax adjustments and any other relief. When interest is claimed in a commercial suit, Order VII Rule 2A CPC calls for the basis, rate, starting date, end date, aggregate interest up to calculation and daily accrual thereafter. Section 12 of the Commercial Courts Act separately governs calculation of specified value.

A buyer's allegation of defective goods, non-performance, retention or set-off must be recorded against the relevant invoice. Do not use a single ledger balance to obscure disputed items.

8. Document Limitation, Acknowledgements and Part-Payments

Prepare a separate chronology showing the contractual due date, alleged breach, invoice dates, delivery or completion, part-payments, acknowledgements, demand notices, mediation dates and any prior proceeding. The applicable limitation period and commencement point depend on the cause of action and governing law; a general commercial debt label does not settle the question.

Under Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963, a qualifying written and signed acknowledgement made before expiry of the prescribed period may start a fresh period. Section 19 deals with qualifying payment made before expiry and the statutory requirements for its acknowledgement. A reminder, informal negotiation or accounting entry does not automatically extend limitation. The effect of any acknowledgement or payment requires case-specific analysis.

Also examine any applicable statutory exclusion period, including the period spent in qualifying Section 12A mediation. Record the method used for limitation calculation so it can be checked before institution.

Final Commercial-Suit Filing Checklist

  • Commercial-dispute classification, specified value and competent court checked.
  • Arbitration, jurisdiction and contractual notice clauses checked.
  • Section 12A mediation record available where required, or genuine urgent-interim-relief facts documented.
  • Correct parties, institution authority, plaint and Statement of Truth prepared.
  • Complete Order XI list and required document copies prepared, including material adverse records.
  • Invoice-wise reconciliation, payment appropriation, interest and relief calculations checked.
  • Electronic originals and applicable certification evidence preserved.
  • Limitation chronology and acknowledgements independently reviewed.
  • Court fees, index, copies, translations and e-filing requirements checked against local rules.

Common Questions

Are invoices and a ledger enough to file and prove a commercial recovery claim?

Not necessarily. The court must be given a legally sufficient pleaded cause of action and the required disclosure. Invoices and ledger entries should be reconciled with contracts, supply or service evidence, payments and disputed communications.

Must every commercial suit undergo mediation first?

Section 12A ordinarily applies to commercial suits that do not contemplate urgent interim relief. The nature of the claim, relevant exceptions and actual record should be checked before institution.

Can relevant documents be produced after the plaint is filed?

Order XI contains specific provisions and restrictions concerning later production, including special treatment of urgent filing. Omission without adequate justification may affect whether the court permits reliance on a document.

Statutes, Rules and Judgments

  • Commercial Courts Act, 2015: Sections 2, 3, 12, 12A and the Schedule amending the CPC.
  • Order XI Rule 1 CPC (commercial disputes): disclosure, list particulars and documents in custody.
  • Order VI Rule 15A CPC: Statement of Truth; and Order VII Rule 2A CPC: interest particulars.
  • Commercial Courts (Pre-Institution Mediation and Settlement) Rules, 2018, subject to later applicable amendments and rules.
  • Patil Automation Pvt. Ltd. v. Rakheja Engineers Pvt. Ltd., Supreme Court, 17 August 2022 (Section 12A).
  • Yamini Manohar v. T.K.D. Keerthi, Supreme Court, 13 October 2023 (urgent interim relief).
  • Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, Sections 61-63, where applicable; Limitation Act, 1963, Section 18 and Section 19.
