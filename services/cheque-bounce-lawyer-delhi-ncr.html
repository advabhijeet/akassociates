Cheque Bounce Lawyer in Delhi NCR: Section 138 Jurisdiction and Procedure

Delhi, Noida, Ghaziabad, Gurugram and Faridabad fall within different court jurisdictions. A cheque dishonour complaint is not necessarily filed where a party resides, the contract was signed, or the cheque was physically deposited. The bank-account branch, presentation route, statutory dates and earlier case record require examination first.

Delhi NCR Location And Forum Record

A Delhi NCR cheque dispute is not automatically a Delhi court case

Chambers of AK is based in Patna. This page provides information for document-led enquiries involving Delhi NCR parties and transactions, including the National Capital Territory of Delhi, Noida and Ghaziabad in Uttar Pradesh, and Gurugram and Faridabad in Haryana. Delhi NCR is not a single court jurisdiction. The page does not imply a Delhi office or an automatic right to file every NCR-connected case in Delhi.

The first task in a proposed Section 138 complaint is to identify the statutory offence, the correct parties, the cheque's route through the banking system, and the Magistrate court competent under Section 142(2) of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881. A Delhi business transaction may involve a payee's bank account in Noida; the drawer may be in Gurugram while the relevant account branch is elsewhere. These facts cannot be reduced to a generic regional service keyword.

Forum Selection

Section 142(2) NI Act: two different banking routes

Section 142(2) is the starting point for territorial jurisdiction in a Section 138 prosecution. The applicable rule depends on whether the cheque was delivered for collection through an account or presented otherwise than through an account.

Section 142(2)(a): cheque collected through an account

Where the cheque is delivered for collection through the payee's or holder-in-due-course's account, jurisdiction ordinarily follows the branch where the payee or holder maintains that account. The Explanation deems delivery at another branch of the payee's bank to have occurred at the account-maintaining branch for jurisdictional purposes.

Section 142(2)(b): presented otherwise than through an account

Where presentation is otherwise than through an account, the provision points to the branch of the drawee bank where the drawer maintains the account. The original instrument, banking endorsements and presentation records should be reviewed before this different route is applied.

Bridgestone India Pvt. Ltd. v. Inderpal Singh (2015) examined the effect of the territorial-jurisdiction amendment. Prakash Chimanlal Sheth v. Jagruti Keyur Rajpopat, 2025 INSC 897, reiterated the account-maintaining-branch rule even though the cheque was deposited for collection at another branch.

In Jai Balaji Industries Ltd. v. HEG Ltd., 2025 INSC 1362 (28 November 2025), the Supreme Court clarified the payee's home-branch rule and the statutory deemed-delivery Explanation. A payee cannot change the court merely by depositing an account-payee cheque at a convenient branch of the same bank in another city. Bank confirmation of the relevant account branch can therefore be more useful than a deposit slip identifying only the physical collection counter.

The correct local Magistrate court or district still needs to be identified once the governing branch is known. These illustrations are not instructions to file in a named court. Cheques presented otherwise than through an account require the separate Section 142(2)(b) analysis.

Identifying the Delhi court: If the statutory banking analysis places jurisdiction within the NCT of Delhi, use the actual branch location and the current district or court allocation to identify the competent Magistrate. The official Delhi District Courts directory provides district websites, notices and court information. Do not assume that all Delhi complaints belong at one court complex or infer a particular Magistrate solely from a postal city name.

Existing Complaints

Multiple cheques, the same drawer and Section 142A

Do not treat every new dishonoured cheque in a continuing relationship as a completely independent court-selection exercise. Section 142A(2) addresses subsequent complaints by the same payee or holder in due course against the same drawer where an earlier qualifying complaint is pending in a court with jurisdiction under Section 142(2). The statutory conditions and the first pending complaint must be checked before a second complaint is filed elsewhere.

Preserve the earlier complaint number, court, drawer's legal identity, cheque dates, orders returning or transferring the matter, and any consolidation or transfer directions. A live proceeding may require a different jurisdictional step from a fresh filing.

Statutory Dates

Cheque validity, demand notice and Section 142 limitation

Correct territorial jurisdiction does not cure a late or defective statutory demand. Section 138 requires a cheque linked to a legally enforceable liability, presentation within its valid period, dishonour, a written demand, receipt or legally effective service and failure to pay within the statutory opportunity.

Cheque validity: For contemporary cheques, the Reserve Bank of India's directions generally require presentation within three months from the date of the instrument. The RBI's 4 November 2011 circular made the three-month banking rule effective from 1 April 2012. Section 138 proviso (a) refers to presentation within six months or the period of validity, whichever is earlier; the shorter RBI validity period therefore matters in practice. Always check the instrument date and relevant banking records.

Cheque and bank records

  • Cheque date, amount, account holder and beneficiary
  • Presentation date and collection route
  • Payee's account-maintaining branch and bank proof
  • Return memo, reason for dishonour and date of bank information

Demand notice and complaint dates

  • Ordinarily written demand within 30 days after bank information
  • Written demand clearly identifying the cheque amount; preserve the issued notice and dispatch record
  • Dispatch, actual receipt, refusal or deemed service
  • 15 days following notice receipt to make payment
  • Complaint ordinarily within one month after the cause of action arises; statutory condonation may apply

Court and settlement record

  • Any existing Section 138 complaint against the same drawer
  • Summons, appearance, trial or appellate order
  • Payments, credits, settlements and cheque-wise outstanding amount
  • Accurate limitation calculations from primary records

The statutory requirement is a notice in writing demanding payment; the Act does not state that an otherwise identifiable notice is automatically invalid merely because it lacks a signature. In practice, keep an authenticated issued copy, proof of authorship, dispatch and legally effective service, and evaluate any actual notice defect on its facts.

Exact dates should be computed from the evidence, not a generic online calculator. A refusal, disputed service, repeated presentation, part-payment or intervening holiday may need specific legal treatment. For a separate step-by-step notice audit, see our Section 138 notice, service and limitation guide.

Evidence Review

Complainant and accused-side document checklists

Payee / complainant

  • Original cheque, deposit slip and return memo
  • Bank certificate, account statement and home-branch proof
  • Loan, invoice, delivery, settlement or other liability papers
  • Complete statutory notice and delivery evidence
  • Cheque-wise reconciled balance and part-payment schedule
  • Company or partnership authority to institute the complaint

Drawer / accused

  • Notice, envelope, summons, complaint and all court orders
  • Cheque-book and bank statement history
  • Proof of payments, settlement and credits
  • Evidence concerning security purpose or disputed liability
  • Bank branch evidence relevant to forum objections
  • Role, resignation and authority records if Section 141 is invoked

Sections 118 and 139 provide rebuttable presumptions when their statutory conditions are met; evidence-based transaction analysis matters for both sides. Where a firm or company is the drawer, Section 141 requires separate examination of the entity, signatory and alleged responsibility of each proposed accused. Merely holding the designation of director does not alone establish vicarious criminal liability.

The correct statutory notice demand is also significant. In Kaveri Plastics v. Mahdoom Bawa Bahrudeen Noorul, 2025 INSC 1133, the Supreme Court considered a notice that incorrectly demanded twice the cheque amount. The statutory sum should be unambiguous, and separate demands should not obscure it.

Settlement and Appearance

Compounding, settlement and Delhi NCR court-stage planning

Section 147 makes NI Act offences compoundable. Settlement terms should identify the specific cheques and complaints, amount, payment milestones, default consequences, timing of the compounding application and any connected recovery claims. Do not assume negotiations suspend notice, filing or appearance requirements.

Interim compensation and appeal: Section 143A permits the trial court, in specified circumstances, to direct interim compensation of up to 20% of the cheque amount; it is discretionary, not automatic. Section 148 concerns a deposit by a convicted drawer in an appeal against conviction, ordinarily with a statutory minimum of 20% of the trial court's fine or compensation if a deposit is ordered; the Supreme Court recognises exceptions where reasons are recorded. Neither provision substitutes for examining the actual orders, liabilities and procedural stage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Delhi NCR cheque-bounce jurisdiction FAQs

Can a Section 138 complaint be filed in Delhi if the cheque was deposited in Delhi?

Not merely for that reason. If collected through the payee's account, Section 142(2)(a) ordinarily points to the branch where the payee maintains that account. A deposit at another branch of the same bank does not by itself change jurisdiction. Check Section 142A for any earlier pending complaint.

Does a drawer living in Noida or Gurugram automatically decide the court?

No. Residence or business address alone ordinarily does not determine Section 138 territorial jurisdiction. The banking route under Section 142(2) and any relevant Section 142A consequence must be reviewed.

What are the main Section 138 notice and complaint periods?

The written demand ordinarily must be made within 30 days of receiving the bank's information about dishonour. The drawer then has 15 days after receipt of the notice to pay. Section 142 ordinarily requires the complaint within one month after the cause of action arises, subject to statutory condonation.

Enquiry Preparation

What to include when requesting a document review

Provide the cheque number and amount, names of the drawer and payee, precise bank and account-maintaining branch, how the cheque was presented, return memo and bank information date, underlying debt record, notice and proof of service, payments and settlement history, and any complaint number, court or summons. Identify whether the enquiry concerns proposed filing, territorial jurisdiction, notice, defence, settlement or appeal.
